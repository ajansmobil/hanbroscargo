How should you plan a move abroad?

The easiest way to run an international move smoothly is to split the work into eight weeks, counting back from moving day: decisions and quotes first, customs paperwork and decluttering in the middle, and packing and collection at the end. The plan below lists what to do each week; if you have less time, keep the same order and simply compress the steps.

The 8-week plan at a glance

WeekFocus
Week 8Decisions, budget and volume estimate
Week 7Surveys and written quotes
Week 6Choose a company, fix dates, start customs paperwork
Week 5Declutter, sell and donate
Week 4Utilities, change of address and official tasks
Week 3Start packing, build the inventory
Week 2Finish packing, prepare white goods
Week 1Final checks and collection day
ArrivalDelivery, checks and settling in

Week 8: make the key decisions

  • Set your moving date and the earliest date your goods need to arrive.
  • Check the official timetable: visas, residence permits, and job or school start dates.
  • Draft a rough list of what you are taking and use our Smart Calculation tool for a first estimate of volume and weight.
  • Agree a budget range for the move.

Week 7: surveys and quotes

  • Ask international removals companies for a survey and a written quote.
  • Compare quotes on packing, customs, insurance, delivery terms and storage, not just price.
  • Weigh up part load against full load based on your volume and how flexible your dates are.

Week 6: choose a company and start the paperwork

  • Choose your removals company and confirm the collection date and delivery plan in writing.
  • If you need a customs relief application (for example ToR1 in the UK), check the conditions on official sources and start gathering documents.
  • Find out the insurance options and how values should be declared.
  • Research parking, lifts and building rules at the delivery address, and ask the local council about temporary parking permits if needed.

Week 5: declutter

  • Go room by room and set aside anything you will not take.
  • List items for sale and separate those to donate.
  • Remove anything your new home already has, such as furniture in a furnished let or a fitted kitchen.
  • Photograph your valuables and keep their receipts together in one folder.

Week 4: official tasks and utilities

  • Plan closing dates for electricity, water, gas, internet and phone contracts.
  • Tell your bank, insurers and other organisations about your change of address.
  • Collect school records, medical records and prescriptions.
  • Complete your customs relief application, or make sure it is nearly done.

Week 3: start packing

  • Begin with things you rarely use: books, seasonal clothes and decorations.
  • Number every box and record the number, room and contents on the inventory.
  • If you are using professional packing, agree which items the crew will pack.
  • Keep the documents and valuables you will carry yourself in a separate place.

Week 2: finish the rest

  • Plan which furniture to dismantle and keep screws in labelled bags.
  • Start running down the kitchen so the fridge and freezer can be emptied.
  • Find or obtain the transit bolts for your washing machine.
  • Make sure the inventory is in English or the language your destination requires.
  • Confirm the collection time, address and access details with your removals company.

Week 1: final checks and collection day

  • Unplug, defrost and dry the fridge.
  • Pack your first-night box and your suitcase.
  • On collection day, check the inventory with the crew and keep a copy.
  • Photograph the empty home, take meter readings and arrange the key handover.

Arrival: delivery and settling in

  • On delivery, check the boxes against the inventory and note any visible damage on the paperwork before signing, with photos.
  • Decide in advance which furniture goes in which room, and use the optional assembly service if you have booked it.
  • Complete your first tasks in the new country, such as address registration, a bank account and health registration.

Costs people forget to budget for

Moving budgets are often built around the removals quote alone, yet other costs affect the total too. Add these to your list at the start:

  • Boxes and packing materials if you are packing yourself
  • Cleaning at the old and new homes
  • Temporary accommodation or a furnished let until your belongings arrive
  • Storage, if needed
  • Parking permits or council fees for delivery day
  • Furniture assembly, curtains and light fittings
  • Duty and VAT on items not covered by relief
  • The insurance premium and the policy excess
  • Plug adaptors, white goods or a kitchen in your new country

Seeing these costs from the outset avoids surprises in the final weeks and makes it easier to decide what to ship and what to buy in your new home.

Moving with children

For children, moving abroad brings uncertainty as well as excitement, and involving them makes the change easier. Let each child pack their own box and make sure it is among the first to be opened. Carry school reports, vaccination records and medical documents with you, as schools in the new country may ask for them. If possible, look at photos of the new city, school or home together before the move, and keep familiar toys and books in the suitcase for the first weeks.

If you have less time

If you do not have eight weeks, the order still applies; simply merge the weeks. Always prioritise three things: measuring your volume and getting a written quote, starting customs paperwork early and fixing the collection date. Handing the packing to a professional crew is the single biggest time-saver on a tight schedule.

Frequently Asked Questions

How far ahead should I contact a removals company?

The earlier, the more date options you will have. This plan suggests starting conversations about two months before you move.

When should I start the customs paperwork?

As soon as you have chosen your company. Relief applications and gathering documents can take time and should be finished before your goods reach the border.

What if my belongings arrive before I do?

If nobody can receive them, they can be held in storage until your delivery date. This is planned when you get your quote.

Do I need to be at home on collection day?

We recommend that you, or someone you authorise, are present to check the inventory with the crew and sign the paperwork.

When should I start packing?

This plan suggests starting with rarely used items about three weeks before the move. If you have booked professional packing, the crew usually comes close to collection day, so you can focus on decluttering in the final weeks.

Which countries does this plan work for?

It is a general framework that works in the same order for moves to the UK, Germany, the Netherlands, France, Belgium, Luxembourg and Austria. Check country-specific document requirements separately.

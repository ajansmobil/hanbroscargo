How do you ship commercial goods from Turkey to the UK?

To ship commercial goods from Turkey to the UK you need a properly prepared commercial invoice and packing list, the correct commodity code (HS code) for each product, a GB EORI number for the UK importer and clear Incoterms that set out who pays the taxes. Your shipping model, whether bulk delivery to a warehouse or parcels direct to consumers, then shapes both the cost and the tax process.

Two main shipping models

1. Bulk shipments to a UK warehouse (B2B)

Goods travel on pallets or in cartons, as a part load or a full load, to a warehouse, fulfilment centre or business customer in the UK. Customs clearance happens once per shipment, and the goods are then distributed to customers from within the UK. For sellers with regular, sizeable sales, this is usually the most efficient model.

2. Parcels direct to consumers (B2C)

Each order is sent as an individual parcel from Turkey straight to the customer in the UK. It keeps stock risk low at the start, but every parcel needs its own declaration and tax treatment, and returns are harder to manage.

Documents you will need

  • Commercial invoice: seller and buyer details, product description, quantity, unit price, total value, currency, country of origin and Incoterms, all clearly stated.
  • Packing list: the contents, weight and dimensions of every carton or pallet.
  • Commodity code: the correct HS code for each product. UK duty rates depend on it, and you can check codes with the UK Trade Tariff tool on gov.uk.
  • EORI number: whoever acts as importer in the UK needs an EORI number starting with GB.
  • Proof of origin: to benefit from the UK–Turkey trade agreement, you need evidence that the goods meet the rules of origin.
  • Product-specific documents: some categories, such as food, cosmetics, electrical goods and toys, carry extra paperwork and compliance requirements.

Taxes: import duty and import VAT

Commercial goods entering the UK face two main charges: customs duty, set by the commodity code and origin, and import VAT. Under the UK–Turkey trade agreement, goods that meet the rules of origin may qualify for preferential (reduced or zero) duty, while VAT is calculated separately.

There is a specific rule for low-value consignments: for goods worth £135 or less sold directly to consumers, VAT is collected at the point of sale rather than at the border. That means the seller must register for UK VAT, or the sale must go through an online marketplace that collects the VAT itself. For consignments above £135, import VAT is paid on import. Check the current details and exceptions with HMRC on gov.uk.

Incoterms: DAP or DDP?

Incoterms decide who handles customs and pays the taxes:

IncotermWho pays the taxes?Things to watch
DAP (Delivered at Place)The buyerThe buyer faces duty and clearance charges on arrival, which can frustrate consumers.
DDP (Delivered Duty Paid)The sellerThe seller must be able to act as UK importer, with a GB EORI and, where needed, UK VAT registration.

We recommend reviewing which option suits your business model with a customs broker.

Packing and pallet preparation

  • Use standard pallets (the Euro pallet, common across Europe, measures 120 × 80 cm) and do not let goods overhang the edges.
  • Secure cartons with stretch film and use corner protectors.
  • Mark every carton with the product code, quantity and carton number, matching the packing list exactly.
  • If you are delivering to a warehouse or marketplace fulfilment centre, check its labelling and booking-in rules in advance.

Delivering to marketplace warehouses

For sellers supplying the UK warehouses of large online marketplaces, warehouse rules are decisive: carton and pallet sizes, barcode and label standards and delivery appointments all vary by platform. Check the current requirements in your seller account before planning a shipment, and share them with your logistics provider.

Plan returns from the start

Returns are inevitable in e-commerce, and in cross-border selling they are the cost most often overlooked. Bringing an item returned by a UK customer back to Turkey means a fresh export and import. Many sellers therefore collect returns at an address or warehouse in the UK, inspect them there and either relist them or send them back in batches. Set the returns terms in your sales policy so they meet UK consumer rights and are workable from a logistics point of view.

Start small, then scale

For sellers new to the UK market, it helps to treat the first shipment as a trial. A first consignment with a limited range of products shows whether your paperwork, commodity codes, labels and warehouse booking-in all work as they should. Once any gaps are fixed, you can standardise your invoice and packing list templates and increase shipment frequency and volume in line with demand. Keeping the same document format on regular shipments also makes customs clearance more predictable.

Product compliance

Products sold in the UK must meet safety and labelling rules. Categories such as electrical goods, toys and cosmetics have extra requirements, including conformity markings, English labels and instructions, and in some cases a UK-based responsible person. Check the rules for your product category on gov.uk before you ship.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do I need an EORI number to ship commercial goods to the UK?

Yes. Whoever acts as the importer in the UK needs an EORI number starting with GB. Depending on the Incoterms, that may be the buyer or the seller.

What is the £135 rule?

For goods worth £135 or less sold directly to consumers, VAT is collected at the point of sale. Check the HMRC guidance on gov.uk for details and exceptions.

Is there zero duty on goods made in Turkey?

Many goods that meet the rules of origin under the UK–Turkey trade agreement may qualify for preferential rates, but this depends on the commodity code and proof of origin. VAT is calculated separately.

Which method suits small, regular shipments?

For regular but modest volumes, palletised part loads are usually the most cost-effective. As volumes grow, a full load becomes worth considering.

How long does a shipment take?

It depends on the route, the transport method and customs. An estimated timeframe is confirmed in writing with your quote.

Shipping Household Goods to the UK After Brexit: ToR1 Relief Explained

What is ToR1 relief?

ToR1 (Transfer of Residence) relief lets people moving to the UK from abroad bring their used personal and household belongings in without paying import duty or import VAT. Broadly, you must have lived outside the UK for at least 12 months, have owned and used the goods for the required period, and apply online before your belongings arrive.

What changed after Brexit?

Since the UK left the European Union, every household goods shipment arriving from Turkey or the EU must be covered by a customs declaration. Whether your goods travel in a dedicated vehicle or as a part load (groupage), they must be declared at the border. ToR1 is the legal basis that allows that declaration to be made free of tax. Without an approved application, or if the conditions are not met, import duty and import VAT may be charged on the value of your goods.

Who can claim ToR1?

The general conditions published by HMRC are summarised below. Details can vary with your circumstances, so always read the current guidance on gov.uk before applying.

  • Residence abroad: you must have lived outside the UK for at least 12 consecutive months before moving.
  • Ownership and use: you must have owned and used the goods outside the UK for the required period (generally at least six months).
  • Genuine move: you must be moving your normal place of residence to the UK.
  • Time limit: the goods must arrive within the permitted window (generally within 12 months of your move).
  • No disposal: goods imported under relief must not be sold, hired out, lent or otherwise transferred for a set period after import.

What is covered, and what is not?

ToR1 covers used goods brought in for personal use. Furniture, clothing, books, kitchenware, electronics and private vehicles that meet the conditions usually fall within it. Some items are excluded:

  • Alcohol and tobacco products
  • Commercial vehicles and equipment for business use
  • Goods intended for sale or trade
  • New, unused items (these may be taxed separately)

Separately from the relief, some goods are banned or restricted on entry to the UK, such as weapons, certain plants and seeds, and foods such as meat and dairy products. The safest approach is to leave food out of your household shipment altogether.

Applying for ToR1 step by step

  1. Check eligibility: read the residence and ownership conditions on gov.uk.
  2. Gather evidence: prepare digital copies of documents showing your residence abroad and your move to the UK.
  3. Finalise the inventory: complete an English inventory, prepared with your removals company, that matches the box numbers.
  4. Apply online: submit the ToR01 application on gov.uk before your goods reach the UK.
  5. Share the reference: once HMRC approves your application, send the ToR reference number to your removals company; the customs declaration is made using it.

Which documents are needed?

The evidence requested varies from person to person, but most people start with:

  • Your passport and, where relevant, your UK visa or proof of your right to reside
  • Proof of residence abroad (for example a tenancy agreement, utility bills or a letter from your employer)
  • Proof of settling in the UK (for example a UK tenancy agreement, title deeds or an employment contract)
  • Proof of ownership and length of use for valuable items and vehicles
  • A detailed inventory in English

If your documents are in Turkish, check during the application whether HMRC requires translations.

Common mistakes

  • Applying late: if the reference number is not ready when your goods reach the port or border, the shipment may be held and extra costs can arise.
  • A vague inventory: describe box contents clearly instead of using phrases like “miscellaneous items”.
  • Mixing in new goods: furniture or appliances bought just before the move may not meet the use condition; list them separately.
  • Adding commercial goods: items for sale are outside ToR1 and need a separate commercial declaration.

How to write a good inventory

The inventory used for your ToR1 application and customs declaration should be clear enough for a customs officer to understand what is being moved without opening a box. The examples below show the difference:

VagueClear
Box 12: miscellaneousBox 12 – Kitchen: plates, glasses, cutlery (used)
Box 27: electronicsBox 27 – Living room: television, set-top box, cables (used)
Furniture3-seat sofa, dining table and 6 chairs (used)

Each line should include the box number, the room and a summary of contents, with used and new items listed separately. Customs may examine the shipment to check that the boxes match the declaration. Boxes that do not match the inventory can lead to delays and extra questions, so the safest approach is to fill in the list box by box as you pack.

Final check before you apply

  • Do your moving date and the date your goods enter the UK fall within the time limit?
  • Is your evidence of residence abroad and length of use ready?
  • Are new and used items on separate lines of the inventory?
  • Are ownership documents for valuables and any vehicle in your file?
  • Does your removals company know when and how you will send the reference number?

If you can answer “yes” to all five, you are ready to apply.

Bringing a vehicle?

Your private car may also qualify under ToR1, provided it meets the ownership and use conditions. Registering the vehicle in the UK and making it road-legal after customs is a separate process. If you plan to ship a vehicle, we can look at these steps together when preparing your quote.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is there a fee for a ToR1 application?

HMRC does not charge an application fee for ToR01 applications made on gov.uk. Any customs service charge from your removals company is shown separately in your quote.

When should I apply for ToR1?

As early as possible, and before your goods arrive in the UK. Without a reference number, the declaration cannot be made free of tax.

Does being a Turkish citizen affect ToR1?

The relief depends on a change of residence, not on nationality. Anyone who meets the conditions can apply; check your own circumstances against the gov.uk guidance.

What happens if ToR1 is refused?

Import duty and import VAT may be charged on your goods. In that case, the declaration and payment are planned with your removals company's customs team.

Can I send my belongings in more than one shipment?

This may be possible, but each shipment must arrive within the time limit and the reference must be used correctly on each declaration. Confirm the details before you apply.

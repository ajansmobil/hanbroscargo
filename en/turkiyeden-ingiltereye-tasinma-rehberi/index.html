Moving from Turkey to the UK: A Step-by-Step Guide

How do you move from Turkey to the UK?

Moving from Turkey to the UK comes down to four steps: confirm your residence status and moving dates, work out the volume of what you are taking, prepare your customs paperwork (in particular a ToR1 relief application) and agree door-to-door transport in a written quote. This guide walks through each step in order, with practical tips along the way.

Step 1: Fix your dates and priorities

Start by deciding when your belongings actually need to arrive in the UK. A job start date, the school term or the beginning of a tenancy will usually set this. Visas and immigration are a separate process from your removal, so check the current requirements on gov.uk. When you speak to a removals company, give two dates clearly: the earliest your goods can be collected in Turkey and the earliest you can receive them in the UK.

Your belongings will often arrive after you do, so plan a suitcase for the first few weeks: clothes for several days, key documents, medication, chargers and a few familiar things for the children.

Step 2: Decide what to take

Taking everything is not always sensible. Because volume drives most of the cost, ask yourself: “Is it better to ship this, or to buy a replacement in the UK?”

  • Worth taking: personal belongings, family heirlooms, good-quality furniture, hand-made rugs, books and kitchenware that is hard to find.
  • Worth reconsidering: ageing white goods, low-value furniture that takes up a lot of space, and large pieces that will not fit your new home.
  • Electrical items: mains voltage is similar, but the UK uses Type G sockets while Turkey uses Type F. Small appliances will need an adaptor or a plug change.
  • Rented homes: many UK lets come furnished. Check your tenancy agreement before shipping a full house of furniture into a furnished flat.

Step 3: Measure the volume and get a written quote

International removals are priced mainly on volume (m³) and weight. Our Smart Calculation tool lets you add your items to a list and see an initial estimate of total volume and weight. The final figure is confirmed during a survey, when our team checks your belongings in person and notes anything fragile or in need of special care.

When comparing quotes, read the scope line by line rather than looking only at the total:

  • Are packing materials and labour included?
  • Who handles export clearance in Turkey and import clearance in the UK?
  • What type of insurance is offered, and what does it cover?
  • Have floors, stairs and vehicle access at the delivery address been taken into account?
  • Can furniture assembly and removal of packing waste be added as options?
  • Is storage available if your goods need to wait before delivery?

Step 4: Prepare customs paperwork

Since Brexit, every household goods shipment entering the UK needs a customs declaration. If you have lived outside the UK for at least 12 consecutive months and have owned and used your belongings for the required period, Transfer of Residence (ToR1) relief can let you bring them in without paying import duty or import VAT. You apply online and pass the reference number you receive to your removals company. We cover the conditions in detail in our ToR1 guide; always confirm the current rules with HMRC on gov.uk.

Documents you will typically need include:

  • Your passport and evidence of your right to live in the UK
  • Your UK address (tenancy agreement, title deeds or a letter from your employer)
  • An English-language inventory that matches the numbered boxes
  • Your ToR1 reference number, if you have applied

Export formalities in Turkey are handled through a customs broker; confirm the current document list with your removals company and the Turkish Ministry of Trade.

Step 5: Packing and collection day

Professional packing uses materials suited to each item: corner protectors for furniture, dedicated boxes for glassware and protective wrapping for electronics. Every box is numbered and recorded on the inventory as it is packed. If you are packing yourself, label boxes with the room and a number, put heavy items in small boxes and prepare a “first night” box. On collection day, check the inventory together with the crew and keep a copy.

Step 6: The journey and the border

Road shipments cross Europe to the Channel and enter the UK through crossings such as Dover or Folkestone. A complete, accurate customs declaration is the single biggest factor in avoiding delays at the border. Transit time depends on the route, the service (part load or full load) and customs, so an estimated timeframe is confirmed when you receive your quote.

Step 7: Delivery in the UK

Narrow streets and parking restrictions, especially in London, can affect delivery. Many councils allow you to suspend a parking bay for moving day, so check your local council's website in advance. Han Bros Cargo's London office makes local coordination easier and can arrange smaller vehicles for tight streets where needed. On delivery, check the boxes against the inventory, note any visible damage on the delivery paperwork and take photos.

Your first tasks after arrival

  • Open a bank account and update your address with the organisations you deal with
  • Apply for a National Insurance number if you will be working
  • Register with a local GP
  • Set up your council tax account
  • Follow up school applications through your local council

Frequently Asked Questions

How long does a move from Turkey to the UK take?

It depends on the route, whether you choose a part load or a full load, and customs. An estimated timeframe based on your dates is given in writing with your quote.

Will I pay customs duty when moving to the UK?

If you meet the ToR1 conditions, your personal and household belongings can enter without import duty or import VAT. Goods that do not qualify may be taxed, so confirm the current rules on gov.uk.

Can my belongings be sent before I arrive?

Yes, provided someone can receive them at a confirmed address. If needed, they can be held in storage until delivery; this is agreed at the quotation stage.

Should I take my white goods to the UK?

They can work in the UK, but the plug type is different and many rentals include appliances. Weigh up the age of the appliance, its volume and what your new home already has.
